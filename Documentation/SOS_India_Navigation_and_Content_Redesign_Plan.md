# SOS India Website Redesign: Navigation and Content Plan

**Prepared:** 30 October 2026  
**Status:** Planning recommendation based on the provided content inventory, DNV menu references, and current project code. This is not approved marketing copy or a certification verification.

## 1. Executive recommendation

Use DNV as a reference for the *way the menu helps people explore*—clear top-level choices, grouped links, a large readable panel, and a useful featured action. Do not copy DNV's industry breadth or its labels. SOS India has a focused maritime offer, so the navigation should organize what the company actually does around vessel operations, ship recycling, training, trust, and useful information.

Recommended desktop navigation:

**SOS India logo (Home) | Services | Training | Insights | About SOS | Request a Quote**

The first four items open structured menus. **Request a Quote** remains a persistent primary action. Certificate verification is a clearly named link inside About SOS and the footer. FAQ is grouped with Insights because it answers service and compliance questions. Careers is placed under About SOS and in the footer; it should not compete with buyer-facing navigation unless hiring becomes a major site goal.

This structure keeps the main header to four exploration choices while retaining the existing high-intent pathways. Every menu destination is grounded in content identified in the inventory; no new business sectors or unverified services are introduced.

## 2. What the references and source content tell us

### DNV examples

- DNV uses a small set of durable top-level choices, then reveals a broad, organized menu only when requested.
- The Services example uses a left-side category selector and a larger right-side panel. This makes a large catalogue scannable without putting every link in the header.
- The Insights example groups content by subject and format. The About example groups corporate, business-area, and media links and gives one featured destination visual prominence.
- The menus keep utility actions separate from discovery. Their value is the information hierarchy and interaction pattern, not the volume of DNV links.

### SOS India content inventory

The source inventory identifies six service categories: IHM, Asbestos Management, Ship Surveys and Inspection, Green Ship Recycling, Technical Management, and Training and Awareness. It also identifies course topics, company and leadership information, claimed credentials, clients/partners, certificate verification, Insights, FAQs, contact points, a quote form, and job openings as an intended managed content type.

The PDF explicitly says it is an extraction baseline rather than approved copy. It flags current credentials, regulatory statements and dates, leadership claims, client logos, third-party references (“TES” and “Tangar shipping”), repeated ship-recycling stages, the quote form's unlabeled field, and app-download details for review. These are editorial gates, not content to silently carry forward.

## 3. Proposed information architecture

### A. Services menu

**Purpose:** Help a shipowner, manager, buyer, or recycling facility identify the relevant capability quickly and enter the correct service detail page.

**Panel heading and action:** “Services” + “View all services”. The heading/action leads to a service index, not to Insights.

| Menu group | Links | Why these belong together |
|---|---|---|
| Vessel operations and compliance | Inventory of Hazardous Materials (IHM); Asbestos Management; Ship Surveys and Inspection; Technical Management | These support vessel documentation, risk, condition, compliance, and operation during a ship's working life. |
| Ship recycling and facilities | Green Ship Recycling; Compliance Monitoring; Ship-Specific Recycling Plan (SSRP); Ship Recycling Facility Plan (SRFP); Dismantling Weight Calculations; Facility Upgrades | These are the distinct vessel-end-of-life and recycling-yard capabilities already named in the inventory. Sub-links should only appear if each has a real page or a useful anchored section. |
| Workforce capability | Training and Awareness | This keeps the training service visible to buyers; a separate Training menu serves people looking for a specific course. |

**Feature card:** Use one evidence-led service story, process diagram, or current service highlight. If no approved case study or image is available, use a text feature such as “Need help choosing a service?” with Request a Quote. Do not use an empty decorative card or invented results.

**Menu behavior:** On desktop, opening Services reveals a wide panel. The left rail has the three groups; the right area shows the active group's links and a small contextual sentence. Hover should not be the only way to operate it; click and keyboard focus must work. “View all services” is always visible.

### B. Training menu

**Purpose:** Serve course seekers and training buyers directly, separate from the broader professional-services catalogue.

**Links:** Course Catalogue; Training Method; Training Topics; Request Group Training.

**Topic links or filters:** IHM awareness; safety induction; hazardous-material handling; fire protection and prevention; oil-spill response; confined-space entry; working at height; mock drills. These should point to actual course pages or catalogue filters only after confirming course availability and details.

**Panel feature:** “Training for ship and shore teams” with a short approved explanation of classroom, practical/onboard, assessment, and feedback methods. Do not advertise certificate validity periods until confirmed.

**Source rationale:** The extraction identifies training as both a service category and a distinct set of courses/topics; the project scope says Courses are CMS-managed. This menu is the learner-facing route, while Services retains the consulting capability and links into training where relevant.

### C. Insights menu

**Purpose:** Reuse the existing content destinations and give readers meaningful ways to browse.

**Links:** Latest Insights; By Topic (IHM and hazardous materials, ship recycling, surveys and safety, maritime compliance); FAQs; Certificate Verification.

**Format links:** Articles, publications/reports, and webinars/videos should only appear if the current Insights CMS actually contains those formats. The source inventory does not establish a complete format catalogue, so do not make these top-level promises yet.

**Feature card:** A recent, published article selected from the current Insights content. If no suitable live article exists, show “Browse all insights” rather than a stale feature.

**Placement rationale:** Insights and FAQs are explicitly identified as existing content to reuse. FAQ belongs here because it helps visitors answer practical service and compliance questions; retain a direct `/faq` route and add cross-links from relevant service pages.

### D. About SOS menu

**Purpose:** Establish who SOS India is and make trust information understandable and verifiable.

**Groups and links:**

- **Company:** About SOS India; Leadership; Locations and Contact.
- **Credentials and verification:** Certifications and Approvals; Verify a Certificate.
- **Work with us:** Careers and Job Openings.

**Feature card:** A real leadership/company image with a short, approved introduction and a “Meet the team” action. Avoid using a certification or client logo as the feature unless its current status and permitted use are confirmed.

**Credentials presentation:** Separate formal certification/approval, training affiliations, client logos, and general partners into clearly labelled groups. Give the verification page a descriptive text link; do not rely on a globe, badge, or unexplained icon.

### E. Persistent action and footer

- Keep **Request a Quote** in the header on desktop and mobile. It should open a dedicated contact/quote route or scroll to a quote form on the same page only when that target is present.
- Include **Contact SOS India** as a supporting action in About SOS and in the footer, with head office, regional office, and training-centre addresses separated and labelled.
- Footer groups: Services; Training; Insights and FAQs; About and Careers; Credentials and Certificate Verification; Contact.
- Preserve email and phone as clickable `mailto:` and `tel:` links.
- Keep the existing quote form's confidentiality message only after legal/editorial review; label every field and state what information is needed.

## 4. Content placement across the redesigned site

### Homepage sequence

1. **Header:** Four exploration menus plus Request a Quote; logo returns home.
2. **Hero:** One concise statement of SOS India's verified maritime focus, paired with two choices: Explore Services and Request a Quote. The existing “Lloyd's Register and IRCLASS Approved…” headline should not ship until the approval wording and scope are confirmed.
3. **Service pathways:** Feature the six service categories. Group the cards visually into “Vessel operations and compliance,” “Ship recycling and facilities,” and “Training,” with a direct route to the full service index. Don't route “View All Services” to Insights.
4. **Who we help / use cases:** Organize only around supported audiences and jobs: vessel owners/managers; ship-recycling facilities; ship and shore training teams. Treat these as audiences, not new sectors or claims.
5. **How engagement works:** A simple approved process (share requirement → scope and quote → delivery and documentation) can help visitors understand how to start, but confirm the actual sales process before publication.
6. **Trust and credentials:** A compact, accurately labelled proof strip with links to credential details and certificate verification. Use only cleared logos and current documentation.
7. **Training preview:** Show available courses/topics and a route to the course catalogue. Keep course cards data-driven from the Courses CMS.
8. **Insights and FAQs:** A small selection of recent published content plus a direct FAQ link; do not duplicate a large content library on the homepage.
9. **Contact/quote section:** Quote form, direct contact information, and clearly labelled locations. The training centre must not be mistaken for the head office.
10. **Footer:** Repeat key destinations, full contact details, verification link, and legal/company details.

### Service detail page template

Use a consistent layout so the menu choice is fulfilled on arrival:

- Breadcrumb: Home → Services → service name.
- Plain-language title, one-sentence outcome, and Request a Quote action.
- “When you need this” / intended client situation.
- Scope and deliverables, using only confirmed service facts.
- Process or lifecycle diagram where it clarifies the work.
- Applicable rules/standards with date and official source, only after verification.
- Related services and relevant Insights/FAQs.
- Credentials that apply to this exact service, with scope stated.
- Contact form with service preselected, if the form supports it.

Service-specific content: IHM should distinguish initial, shipbuilding, maintenance, final Parts I–III, and gap-analysis work; asbestos should distinguish plans, inspection/management, removal, and disposal; surveys should group audits and condition, purchase, pre-vetting, SIRE/OCMF, accident, and incident work; recycling should group monitoring, SSRP, dismantling calculations, SRFP, facility upgrades, and training; technical management should explain the supported operational functions only after ownership/attribution is confirmed.

### Training and course pages

- Training landing page explains audience, delivery methods, available topics, and how to request group training.
- Course detail pages should show audience, learning outcomes, format, duration, prerequisites, location/online availability, assessment, certificate details, and enquiry action where those data are confirmed and available in CMS.
- Make course availability and certificate validity editable/confirmable through the planned Courses content model rather than hard-coded menu copy.

### About, credentials, verification, Insights, and FAQs

- **About:** Company overview, leadership, verified biographies, locations, and Careers.
- **Credentials:** A structured register/gallery with category, issuing body, covered service, scope, validity/status, and a supporting document or verification path when permission allows.
- **Certificate verification:** A utility page reached from About, the footer, and contextually from course/certificate content. Explain required reference fields before asking users to submit them.
- **Insights:** Reuse the existing listing/detail experience and CMS. Add topic taxonomy only where the current data model/content supports it; avoid rebuilding the system.
- **FAQs:** Keep the existing page/CMS. Organize questions under confirmed categories and link to answers from relevant service/course pages.

## 5. Visual and interaction direction from DNV

- Keep a clean, persistent white/light header with a restrained brand palette based on SOS India's existing navy and cyan/blue accents.
- Use a large menu panel with generous spacing, clear columns, short link labels, and a visible section heading. Use a shaded page backdrop only if it preserves content context and passes contrast checks.
- Use one image or content feature only when it adds a useful path; avoid filling empty space with invented categories.
- Show the active top-level item and open/close state clearly. A click toggles each desktop panel; Escape closes it; focus stays predictable; selecting a link closes the panel.
- Mobile uses a full-width accordion/drawer with the same hierarchy, visible Request a Quote action, and no hover-dependent content.
- Every menu link is a real destination. A parent with children should still offer a clearly labelled “View all” route.
- Search and language controls are not recommended for the first release: the provided scope does not establish a site-wide search index or translated content. Add them only when they solve a real supported need.

## 6. Content governance and migration checks

Before writing final content or publishing, the client should confirm:

1. Current LR, IR Class, ISO 9001, UKATA, ClassNK, DISH, and other approval/certification status, exact scope, validity, and logo-use rights.
2. Current regulatory references, effective dates, and any historical timelines/entry-into-force statements.
3. Leadership roles, experience, approved credentials, biographies, and photo permissions.
4. Client/partner logo permissions and the precise relationship represented by each logo.
5. All “TES” and “Tangar shipping” references and any content attributed to third parties.
6. Ship recycling list duplication and the exact service name/sequence; asbestos removal attribution and capabilities.
7. Training catalogue, delivery format, assessment, certificate categories/validity, and course availability.
8. Current office/training-centre addresses, email, telephone, quote form fields, confidentiality/privacy wording, and app download links.
9. Insights and FAQ inventory, current URLs, and any pages to retire or redirect.

Editorial principle: the PDF is a content map, not approval to republish claims. Where proof or scope is pending, keep the item out of live promotional copy and navigation labels until resolved.

## 7. CMS and project implementation fit

The project documentation says Services, Courses, Employees, and Job Openings are intended CMS-managed content types; Insights and FAQs already exist and should be reused; homepage sections otherwise remain predefined/developer-maintained unless scope changes.

Recommended responsibilities:

- **CMS data:** service records, course records, employee records, and job openings according to the approved scope.
- **Existing CMS:** reuse Insights and FAQ APIs, data, routes, and publishing workflow.
- **Developer-maintained structure:** menu groupings and ordering, homepage section order, credential categories/layout, company copy, and contact/footer structure unless the client specifically adds them to CMS scope.
- **Avoid scope creep:** no new CMS for certifications, locations, search, or media formats in the first pass unless a confirmed update workflow requires it.

## 8. Current-code implications to resolve before implementation

- The existing [SiteHeader.tsx](../src/shared/layout/SiteHeader.tsx) currently has Services and Courses as `#services` / `#courses` anchors, Job Openings as `#jobs`, plus Insights and FAQ routes. Those anchors are not a robust cross-page navigation model and should become real links/menu actions.
- The same header's desktop CTA uses `#contact`; confirm a stable contact route/section and ensure it works from Insights, FAQ, and any future service page.
- The homepage currently renders Hero, Services, About, and Training. The PDF's contact, credentials, locations, FAQ, and quote elements need explicit destinations or sections in the final page plan.
- [ServicesSection.tsx](../src/features/home/ServicesSection.tsx) has six service cards, but its “View All Services” currently points to `/insights`. Correct this in the implementation plan and validate every service href against actual routes before release.
- The generated route tree currently needs to be checked against planned service/course/about/contact destinations before building links. The content inventory alone does not establish that those routes already exist.
- Preserve the existing Insights, FAQ, authentication, CMS, approval, and scheduled publishing behavior. Public navigation changes should not require CMS/API/schema changes unless implementation uncovers an actual content-model gap.

## 9. Phased delivery plan

### Phase 1 — Content and destination audit

- Confirm all claims and the checklist in Section 6 with SOS India.
- Inventory live courses, Insights, FAQs, credentials, jobs, contact/quote destinations, and existing public routes.
- Build a link map: current URL → new URL → keep/redirect/retire; identify destination pages that must be created.

### Phase 2 — Sitemap and content model sign-off

- Approve the top-level labels and mega-menu link groups in Section 3.
- Approve service/course page templates and homepage sequence.
- Confirm CMS ownership and exactly which content is editable.

### Phase 3 — Navigation and page implementation

- Implement responsive mega menus with keyboard operation and real routes.
- Build/repair service index and detail pages, course catalogue/detail routes, About/credentials, contact/quote, and certificate verification destinations as needed.
- Reuse existing Insights and FAQ routes/data; add relevant cross-links.
- Ensure every CTA and menu destination resolves correctly and has a useful empty/error state.

### Phase 4 — Content migration and validation

- Migrate approved copy and media; remove or correct duplicate/third-party content.
- Validate all proofs, links, forms, menu keyboard behavior, mobile operation, and redirects.
- Test public pages and confirm Insights/FAQ/CMS workflows still work before any production replacement.

## 10. Success criteria

- A visitor can identify the relevant service within one menu interaction and reach a working detail page.
- A course seeker can reach available courses without navigating the full consulting catalogue.
- FAQs and Insights remain easy to find and continue using their existing content systems.
- Credentials and client logos are accurately labelled, scoped, and linked to verification where applicable.
- Request a Quote and Contact work from every page and on mobile.
- No menu item implies an unsupported sector, service, certification, course, or content format.
- All legacy URLs have an intentional keep, redirect, or retire decision, with no dead anchors in the public header.

## 11. Assumptions and decisions for client review

- “Training” deserves its own top-level route because Courses is a planned CMS content type and has a distinct learner journey; “Training and Awareness” remains visible as a capability in Services.
- FAQs are grouped under Insights & resources, while retaining a direct route and contextual links.
- Certificate verification remains findable under About SOS and in the footer; if verification is a frequent user task, it can also become a small text utility link beside Request a Quote.
- Careers stays secondary to service discovery unless hiring is a priority.
- The exact visual treatment, brand tokens, and card imagery should be finalized after the client approves the information architecture and content claims.
